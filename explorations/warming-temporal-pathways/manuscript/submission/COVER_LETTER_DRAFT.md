**To the Editor,**

Please consider our manuscript, **“Temporal pathways of global lake warming”**,
for publication as an Article in **[TARGET JOURNAL]**.

Global lake-warming assessments commonly report a long-term trend for each
lake. That quantity captures net temperature displacement but leaves unresolved
whether comparable displacement emerged through early, late, strengthening or
weakening periods of local warming. Using reconstructed 1981--2020 annual lake
surface water temperature for 92,245 lakes, we combine forty-year Theil--Sen
slopes with endpoint-aligned trailing ten-year rates. We show that comparable
net warming can have heterogeneous within-period histories across the global
lake population.

The manuscript contributes three linked advances. First, it makes the temporal
history of lake warming a measurable global object rather than a comparison of
one or two chosen periods. Second, it fits low-frequency PCA to occupied
equal-area cells, separating the covariance estimand for a globally balanced
trajectory space from subsequent lake-level projections. The leading temporal
background remains stable across continental and temporal omissions,
representation choices, grid resolutions and sparse-cell thresholds. A
secondary PC2--PC3 timing plane recurs under many perturbations, while its
documented limits prevent it from being presented as two fixed global modes.
Third, the resulting coordinate system provides an interpretable bridge to
ecological comparisons, monitoring design and future heat-budget studies.

The study should interest readers across limnology, climate-change science,
remote sensing, spatial statistics and freshwater ecology. It uses a global
satellite/reanalysis-calibrated temperature reconstruction while explicitly
distinguishing reconstructed surface conditions from in-situ observations. The
manuscript does not claim a circulation mechanism: the retained NAO/AO result
is a geographically robust descriptive association screen that identifies a
specific target for a future attribution design.

All main evidence is supported by a reproducible Julia processing pipeline and
Quarto/R rendering workflow. The manuscript includes a detailed robustness
envelope, panel-level source-data deposit map, and a reporting-summary draft.
The final submission will provide public data and code archive identifiers,
source-data tables, and the journal-required declarations.
